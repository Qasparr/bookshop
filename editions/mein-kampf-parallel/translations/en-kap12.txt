If at the close of this volume I depict the first development-time of our movement and briefly discuss a row of questions conditioned thereby, this does not happen in order to give a treatise about the spiritual goals of the movement. Goals and tasks of the new movement are so mighty that they can be treated only in a volume of their own. So I shall in a second volume discuss thoroughly the programmatic foundations of the movement and try to draw a picture of what we imagine under the word "state". I mean thereby under "us" all the hundred-thousands who in the ground taken desire the same, without in the individual finding the words to depict the inwardly before-eyes-hovering. For it is the remarkable of all great reforms that they at first often possess only a single one as champion, as bearer, however, many millions. Their goal is often already for centuries the inner, longing wish of hundred-thousands, until one throws himself up as proclaimer of such a general willing and, as standard-bearer of the old longing, helps it to victory in a new idea.

[kap12:1]
That, however, millions bear in the heart the wish for a fundamental change of the relations given today proves the deep dissatisfaction under which they suffer. It expresses itself in thousandfold appearance-forms, with the one in faint-heartedness and hopelessness, with the other in aversion, in anger and indignation, with this one in indifference and with that one again in furious excess. As witnesses for this inner dissatisfaction there may count just as much the election-weary as also the many inclining to the most fanatical extreme of the left side.

[kap12:2]
And to these should also the young movement in the first line turn. It is not to form an organization of the satisfied, sated, but it is to gather together the suffering-tormented and peaceless, the unhappy and dissatisfied

[kap12:3]
gather together, and above all it shall not swim on the surface of the peoples-body, but root in the ground of the same.

[kap12:4]
The Position after the Revolution

[kap12:5]
Purely politically taken, there resulted in the year 1918 the following picture: A people is torn into two parts. The one, by far the smaller, comprises the layers of the national intelligence under exclusion of all bodily active. It is outwardly national, but is not able to imagine under this word something other than a very insipid and feeble representation of so-called state interests, which again appear identical with dynastic ones. It tries to champion its thoughts and goals with spiritual weapons, which are just as gapful as superficial, but over against the brutality of the opponent fail in themselves already. With a single frightful blow this, shortly before still ruling, class is struck to the ground and bears in trembling cowardice every humiliation from the side of the ruthless victor.

[kap12:6]
Opposite it stands, as second class, the broad mass of the hand-working population. It is gathered together in more or less radical-Marxist movements, resolved to break every spiritual resistance through the power of force. It does not want to be national, but consciously rejects every furtherance of national interests just as much as, conversely, it gives aid to every foreign oppression. It is numberwise the stronger, but comprises above all those elements of the nation without which a national rising-again is unthinkable and impossible.

[kap12:7]
For about that one had in the year 1918 indeed already to be clear: Every re-ascent of the German people leads only over the regaining of outer power. The preconditions for this, however, are not, as our bourgeois "statesmen" always chatter around, weapons, but the forces of the will. Weapons the German people once possessed more than enough. They have not been able to secure freedom, because the energies of the national self-preservation-urge, the self-preservation-will, were lacking. The best weapon is dead, worthless material so long as the spirit is lacking who is ready, willing and resolved to lead it. Germany became defenceless not because weapons were wanting, but because the will was lacking to preserve the weapon for the folkish continued-preservation.

[kap12:8]
If today particularly our left-sided politicians endeavour to point to weaponlessness as the necessary cause of their will-less, yielding, in truth, however, traitorous policy toward the outside, one must answer them to it only one thing: No, it is right the other way round. Through your

[kap12:9]
anti-national, criminal policy of the giving-up of national interests you once delivered out the weapons; now you try to present the lack of weapons as the grounding cause of your miserable wretchedness. This is, like everything at your doing, lie and falsification.

[kap12:10]
Alone this reproach meets exactly so the politicians of the right. For thanks to their miserable cowardice, in the year 1918 the Jewish rabble come to rule was able to steal the weapons from the nation. Also these therefore have no ground and no right to adduce the today's weaponlessness as compulsion to their clever caution (read "cowardice"), but the defencelessness is the consequence of their cowardice.

[kap12:11]
The Regaining of the Political Power

[kap12:12]
Therewith, however, the question of a regaining of German power is not, say: "How do we fabricate weapons?", but: "How do we produce the spirit which enables a people to bear weapons?" If this spirit rules a people, the will finds a thousand ways, of which every one ends at a weapon! But give a coward ten pistols, and he will at an attack nevertheless not be able to fire off a single shot. They are for him therewith more worthless than for the courageous man a mere cudgel.

[kap12:13]
The question of the regaining of the political power of our people is already therefore in the first line a question of the recovery of our national self-preservation-urge, because every preparatory foreign policy as well as every valuation of a state in itself, according to experience, directs itself less according to the weapons present than according to the recognized or at least suspected moral resistance-capacity of a nation. The alliance-capacity of a people is determined much less through present dead weapon-crowds than through the evident presence of a flaming national self-preservation-will and heroic death-courage. For an alliance is not closed with weapons, but with men. So the English people will have to count as the most valuable ally on the world so long as it, in its leadership and in the spirit of the broad mass, lets that brutality and toughness be expected which are resolved to fight through a once begun struggle, without regard to time and sacrifices, with all means to the victorious end, whereby the momentarily present military armament need stand in no relation to that of other states.

[kap12:14]
If, however, one grasps that the re-elevation of the German nation represents a question of the regaining of our political self-preservation-will, then it is also clear that this is not sufficed by a winning of in themselves already at least according to willing national elements, but only by the nationalization of the consciously anti-national mass.

[kap12:15]
The Winning of the Broad Masses

[kap12:16]
A young movement which sets itself as goal the re-erection of a German state with sovereignty of its own will hence have to set its struggle unrestrictedly onto the winning of the broad masses. However miserable in general our so-called "national bourgeoisie" is, however insufficient its national disposition also appears, so surely is from this side a serious resistance against a forceful national inner and outer policy one day not to be expected. Even if, out of the known narrow-minded-short-sighted grounds, the German bourgeoisie, as already once over against a Bismarck, should in the hour of a coming liberation persist in passive resistance, yet an active resistance against it is, with its acknowledged proverbial cowardice, never to be feared.

[kap12:17]
It behaves otherwise with the mass of our internationally attuned peoples-comrades. They are not only in their primitive original-growthiness more set upon the thought of force, but their Jewish leadership is more brutal and more ruthless. They will strike down every German elevation just as they once broke the backbone of the German army. Above all, however: they will in this parliamentarily ruled state, by force of their majority of number, not only hinder every national foreign policy, but also exclude every higher estimation of German force and therewith every alliance-capacity. For we are conscious of the weakness-moment which lies in our 15 million Marxists, Democrats, Pacifists and Centrists not only ourselves, but it is still more recognized by the foreign country, which measures the value of a possible alliance with us according to the weight of this burden. One does not ally oneself with a state whose active peoples-part stands at least passively over against every resolved foreign policy.

[kap12:18]
To this there comes still the fact that the leadership of these parties of national treason must and will stand hostilely over against every elevation already out of mere self-preservation-urge. It is historically simply not thinkable that the German people could once more take its former position without settling accounts with those who gave the cause and occasion for the unheard-of collapse which visited our state. For before the judgment-seat of posterity November 1918 will be valued not as high-, but as country-treason.

[kap12:19]
So every regaining of a German self-standingness toward the outside is in the first line bound to the regaining of the inner willwise closedness of our people.

[kap12:20]
The Nationalization of the Masses

[kap12:21]
Alone also purely technically considered, the thought of a German liberation toward the outside appears so long as nonsensical as the broad mass is not ready to step into the service of this freedom-thought. Purely militarily seen, it will above all dawn on every officer, at some reflection, that one is not able to lead a struggle toward the outside with student-battalions, but that for it one needs, besides the brains of a people, also the fists. One must thereby further hold before one's eyes that a national defence which supported itself only on the circles of the so-called intelligence would carry on a true robbery-economy at irreplaceable good. The young German intelligence which in the war-volunteer regiments in autumn 1914 found death in the Flanders plain was later bitterly lacking. It was the best good which the nation possessed, and its loss was in the course of the war no more to be replaced. Alone not only the struggle itself is unexecutable if the storming battalions do not see the masses of the workers in their rows, but also the preparation of a technical kind is without the inner willwise unity of our peoples-body unexecutable. Precisely our people, which under the thousand eyes of the peace treaty of Versailles must live on disarmed, is able to make any technical preparations for the attainment of freedom and human independence only then, when the army of inner spies is decimated onto those to whom inborn characterlessness permits to betray everything and each for the known thirty pieces of silver. With these, however, one will finish. Insurmountable, on the contrary, appear the millions who out of political conviction oppose the national elevation — insurmountable so long as the cause of their opponentship, the international Marxist world-view, is not fought and torn out of heart and brain for them.

[kap12:22]
Wholly equal, therefore, from which viewpoint one checks the possibility of the re-attainment of our state and folkish independence — whether from that of the foreign-political preparation, that of the technical armament, or that of the struggle itself — always there remains as precondition to everything the previous winning of the broad mass of our people for the thought of our national self-standingness.

[kap12:23]
Without the re-attainment of the outer freedom, however, every inner reform means even in the most favourable case only the raising of our yield-capacity

[kap12:24]
as colony. The surpluses of every so-called economic lifting come to the benefit of our international control-lords, and every social betterment raises in the most favourable case the work-achievement for these. Cultural advances will to the German nation not at all be granted; they are too much bound to the political independence and dignity of a peoplehood.

[kap12:25]
If, therefore, the favourable solution of the German future is bound to the national winning of the broad mass of our people, then this must also be the highest and mightiest task of a movement whose activity shall not exhaust itself in the satisfaction of the moment, but which has to check all its doing and leaving only at the probable consequences in the future.

[kap12:26]
So we were already in the year 1919 clear about this, that the new movement as highest goal must first carry through the nationalization of the masses. Out of this there resulted in tactical respect a row of demands.

[kap12:27]
1. In order to win the mass for the national elevation, no social sacrifice is too heavy.

[kap12:28]
Whatever economic concessions are made to our employees today, they stand in no relation to the gain of the whole nation if they co-help to give the broad layers back to their peoplehood. Only short-sighted narrow-mindedness, as one unfortunately often finds it in our entrepreneur-circles, can misjudge that there is on the duration no economic ascent for them and therewith also no more economic use, if the inner folkish solidarity of our nation is not restored.

[kap12:29]
If the German trade unions in the war had preserved the interests of the workers in the most ruthless manner, if they even during the war had thousand-times, through strike, pressed off from the then dividend-hungry entrepreneurlot the granting of the demands of the workers represented by them, if, however, they in the concerns of national defence had just as fanatically confessed themselves to their Germandom, and had with equal ruthlessness given to the fatherland what is the fatherland's, then the war would not have been lost. How ridiculous, however,

[kap12:30]
would all and even the greatest economic concessions have been over against the monstrous significance of the won war!

[kap12:31]
So a movement which intends to give the German worker back to the German people has to become clear to itself that economic sacrifices play no role at all in this question, so long as the preservation and independence of the national economy are not threatened through them.

[kap12:32]
2. The national education of the broad mass can only take place over the detour of a social lifting, since exclusively through it those general economic preconditions are created which permit the individual to take part also in the cultural goods of the nation.

[kap12:33]
3. The nationalization of the broad mass can never take place through halfnesses, through weak stressing of a so-called objectivity-standpoint, but through ruthless and fanatically one-sided setting onto the goal now to be striven for. That means then: one cannot make a people "national" in the sense of our today's bourgeoisie, therefore with so-and-so many restrictions, but only nationalistic, with the whole vehemence which is inherent in the extreme. Poison is only broken through counter-poison, and only the shallowness of a bourgeois temper can regard the middle line as the way into the kingdom of heaven.

[kap12:34]
The broad mass of a people consists neither of professors nor of diplomats. The small abstract knowledge which it possesses points its feelings more into the world of feeling. There rests its either positive or negative setting. It is only receptive for a force-expression in one of these two directions and never for a halfness hovering between both. Its feelingwise setting, however, conditions at the same time its extraordinary stability. Belief is harder to shake than knowledge, love is subject less to change than respect, hatred is more lasting than aversion, and the drive-force for the mightiest overturnings on this earth lay at all times less in a scientific knowledge ruling the mass than in a fanaticism ensouling it and sometimes in a hysteria driving it forward.

[kap12:35]
Whoever wants to win the broad mass must know the key which opens the gate to its heart. It is not called objectivity, therefore weakness, but will and force.

[kap12:36]
4. The winning of the soul of the people can only succeed if, beside the leading of the positive struggle for one's own goals, one destroys the opponent of these goals.

[kap12:37]
The people sees at all times in the ruthless attack on an adversary the proof of its own right, and it feels the renunciation of the destruction of the other as uncertainty in regard to its own right, if not as sign of its own un-right.

[kap12:38]
The broad mass is only a piece of nature, and its feeling does not understand the mutual hand-shake of men who claim to want opposite things. What it wishes is the victory of the stronger and the destruction of the weak or his unconditional subjection.

[kap12:39]
The nationalization of our mass will only succeed if, with all the positive struggle for the soul of our people, its international poisoners are exterminated.

[kap12:40]
5. All great questions of the time are questions of the moment and represent only consequence-appearances of determined causes. Causal significance, however, possesses among them all only one: the question of the racial preservation of the peoplehood. In the blood alone lies both the strength and the weakness of man grounded. Peoples which do not recognize and regard the significance of their racial foundation resemble men who would like to teach pugs the properties of greyhounds, without grasping that the swiftness of the greyhound, like the teachableness of the poodle, are not taught-on properties, but ones lying in the race. Peoples which renounce the preservation of their racial purity thereby also perform renunciation of the unity of their soul in all its expressions. The tornness of their being is the nature-necessary consequence of the tornness of their blood, and the change of their spiritual and creative force is only the effect of the change of their racial foundations.

[kap12:41]
Whoever wants to free the German people from its expressions and un-virtues of today, originally foreign to its being, will first have to redeem it from the foreign exciter of these expressions and un-virtues.

[kap12:42]
Without clearest knowledge of the race-problem and therewith of the Jew-question, a re-ascent of the German nation will no more take place.

[kap12:43]
The race-question gives not only the key to world-history, but also to human culture in general.

[kap12:44]
6. The incorporation of the broad mass of our people, standing today in the international camp, into a national peoples-community means no renunciation of the representation of justified estate-interests. Diverging estate- and profession-interests are not equal in significance with class-splitting, but are matter-of-course consequence-appearances of our economic life. The profession-grouping stands in no manner over against a true peoples-community, for this consists in the unity of the peoplehood in all those questions which concern this peoplehood in itself.

[kap12:45]
The incorporation of an estate become class into the peoples-community, or even only into the state, takes place not through the stepping-down of higher classes, but through the lifting-up of the lower ones. Bearer of this process can again never be the higher class, but the lower one fighting for its equal-rightness. Today's bourgeoisie was not incorporated into the state through measures of the nobility, but through its own deed-force under its own leadership.

[kap12:46]
The German worker will not be lifted over the detour of feeble fraternization-scenes into the frame of the German peoples-community, but through conscious lifting of his social and cultural position, so long until the weightiest differences may count as bridged. A movement which sets itself this development as goal will have to fetch its adherentship thereby in the first line out of the worker-camp. It may fall back on intelligence only in the measure in which this has already unrestrictedly grasped the goal to be striven for. This transformation- and approach-process will not be ended in ten or twenty years, but comprises, according to experience, many generations.

[kap12:47]
The heavy obstacle for the approach of today's worker to the national peoples-community lies not in his estatewise interest-representation, but in his international, people- and fatherland-hostile leadership and setting. The same trade unions, led fanatically nationally in political and folkish concerns, would make millions of workers into most valuable members of their peoplehood, without regard to the struggles taking place in the individual in purely economic concerns.

[kap12:48]
A movement which wants to give the German worker in honest manner back to his people and to tear him away from the international delusion must in the

[kap12:49]
sharpest manner make front against an opinion ruling above all in entrepreneur-circles, which under peoples-community understands the resistanceless economic delivery-out of the employee over against the employer, and which in every attempt at the preservation of even justified economic existence-interests of the employee wants to see an attack on the peoples-community. The representation of this opinion represents the representation of a conscious lie; the peoples-community does indeed lay its obligations not only onto the one side, but also onto the others.

[kap12:50]
As surely as a worker sins against the spirit of a real peoples-community if he, without regard to the common welfare and the continuance of a national economy, supported on his power, puts extortionate demands, so much, however, does an entrepreneur also break this community if he, through inhuman and exploiterly kind of his enterprise-leadership, misuses the national work-force and usuries millions out of its sweat. He then has no right to designate himself as national, no right to speak of a peoples-community, but he is an egoistic scoundrel who, through the carrying-in of social unrest, provokes later struggles which so or so must turn out to the damage of the nation. The reservoir out of which the young movement shall draw its adherents will therefore in the first line be the mass of our employees. These it is a question of tearing away from the international delusion, of freeing from their social need, of lifting them out of the cultural misery and of transferring them as a closed, valuable, nationally feeling and nationally willing factor into the peoples-community.

[kap12:51]
If in the circles of the national intelligence there find themselves men with warmest hearts for their people and its future, fulfilled by deepest knowledge for the significance of the struggle for the soul of this mass, they are in the rows of this movement highly welcome as valuable spiritual backbone. A winning of the bourgeois election-vote-cattle, however, may never be the goal of this movement. It would in such a case burden itself with a mass which, according to its whole nature-kind, would bring the recruiting-force over against the broad layers to flagging. For regardless of the theoretical beauty of the thought of a bringing-together of broadest masses from below and above already within the frame of the movement, there stands over against it the fact that one through psychological influencing of bourgeois masses in general proclamations is able well to produce moods, indeed even to spread

[kap12:52]
insight, but not to bring to disappearance character-properties, or better said un-virtues, whose becoming and arising comprised centuries. The difference in regard to the mutual cultural level and the mutual position to the questions of economic concerns is at present still so great that, as soon as the intoxication of the proclamations has passed, it would at once step into appearance as hindering.

[kap12:53]
Finally, however, it is not the goal to undertake a re-layering in the in-itself national camp, but a winning of the anti-national one.

[kap12:54]
And this viewpoint is also finally decisive for the tactical setting of the whole movement.

[kap12:55]
7. This one-sided, but thereby clear, taking of position has also to express itself in the propaganda of the movement and is, on the other side, itself again demanded through propagandistic grounds.

[kap12:56]
If the propaganda for the movement is to be effective, it must turn to one side alone, since in the other case, with the difference of the spiritual pre-formation of the two camps coming into question, it would either not be understood by the one side or be rejected by the other as a matter-of-course and therewith uninteresting.

[kap12:57]
Even the manner of expression and the tone in the individual cannot be equally effective for two so extreme layers. If the propaganda renounces the original-growthiness of the manner of expression, it does not find the way to the feeling of the broad mass. If, on the contrary, it uses in word and gesture the coarseness of the feeling of the mass and of its expressions, it will be rejected by the so-called intelligence as raw and ordinary. Among a hundred so-called speakers there are hardly ten who would be in a position to speak equally effectively today before a public of street-sweepers, locksmiths, canal-clearers, etc., and tomorrow to hold a lecture with necessarily equal thought-content before an auditorium of high-school professors and students. But among a thousand speakers there is perhaps only a single one who manages to speak before locksmiths and high-school professors at once in a form which not only corresponds to both parts in their capacity of conception, but also influences both parts equally effectively or even sweeps them along to the intoxicating storm of applause. One must, however, always hold before one's eyes that even the most beautiful thought of a sublime theory can in most cases find its spreading only through small and smallest spirits. It does not come down to what the genial creator of an idea has in the eye, but to the

[kap12:58]
form and the success with which the proclaimers mediate this idea to the broad mass.

[kap12:59]
The strong recruiting force of Social Democracy, indeed of the whole Marxist movement in general, rested to a great part in the unity and therewith one-sidedness of the public to which it turned. The more seemingly limited, indeed narrow-minded, its trains of thought thereby were, the more easily were they taken up and worked up by a mass whose spiritual level corresponded to that of the brought-forward.

[kap12:60]
Therewith, however, there resulted for this new movement likewise a simple and clear line:

[kap12:61]
The propaganda is in content and form to be set onto the broad mass, and its correctness is exclusively to be measured at its effective success.

[kap12:62]
In a peoples-assembly of the broad layers, not that speaker speaks best who stands spiritually nearest to the intelligence present, but he who conquers the heart of the mass.

[kap12:63]
An intelligence-man present in such an assembly who, in spite of the evident effect of the speaker on the lower layers to be conquered, carps at the speech in regard to the spiritual height, proves the complete inability of his thinking and the worthlessness of his person for the young movement. For it, only he comes into question as intellectual who has already so much grasped task and goal of the movement that he has learned to judge the activity also of the propaganda exclusively according to its success and not according to the impressions which it leaves behind on himself. For the propaganda has not to serve for the entertainment of in themselves already nationally minded men, but for the winning of the enemies of our peoplehood, so far as they are of our blood.

[kap12:64]
In general there should now, for the young movement, those trains of thought which I already briefly gathered together under War Propaganda become determining and decisive for the kind and carrying-through of its own enlightenment-work.

[kap12:65]
That it was right, its success has proven.

[kap12:66]
8. The goal of a political reform-movement will never be reached through enlightenment-work or through influencing of ruling powers, but only through the attainment of political power. Every world-moving idea has not only the right, but the duty, to secure for itself those means which enable the carrying-through of its trains of thought. Success is the

[kap12:67]
sole earthly judge over the right or un-right of such a beginning, whereby under success there is not, as in the year 1918, to be understood the attainment of power in itself, but the effect of the same, blessed for a peoplehood. So a stroke of state is not then to be regarded as succeeded when, as thoughtless state-attorneys in Germany today think, the revolutionaries succeeded in the taking-into-possession of the state power, but only then, when in the realization of the intentions and goals laid as ground of such a revolutionary action there grows for the nation more salvation than under the past regiment. Something which of the German revolution, as the bandit-stroke of autumn 1918 designates itself, cannot well be claimed.

[kap12:68]
If, however, the attainment of political power forms the precondition for the practical carrying-through of reformative intentions, then a movement with reformative intentions must from the first day of its continuance feel itself as movement of the mass and not as literary tea-club or philistine skittle-society.

[kap12:69]
9. The young movement is, according to its being and its inner organization, anti-parliamentary, i.e., it rejects in general, as in its own inner build-up, a principle of majority-determination in which the leader is degraded only to the executor of the will and of the opinion of others.

[kap12:70]
The movement represents in the smallest as in the greatest the basic principle of unconditional leader-authority, paired with highest responsibility.

[kap12:71]
Highest Authority — Highest Responsibility

[kap12:72]
The practical consequences of this basic principle in the movement are the following: The first chairman of a local group is set in by the next-higher leader; he is the responsible leader of the local group. All committees are subject to him and not he conversely to a committee. Voting-committees there are none, but only work-committees. The work the responsible leader, the first chairman, divides in. The same basic principle holds for the next-higher organization, the district, the circle or the Gau. Always the leader is set in from above and at the same time clothed with unlimited proxy and authority. Only the leader of the whole party is, for association-legal grounds, elected in the general members-assembly. He, however, is the exclusive leader of the movement. All committees are subject to him and not he to the committees. He determines and therewith also bears on his shoulders the responsibility. It stands free to the adherents of the movement, before the forum of a new election, to call him to responsibility, to unclothe him of his office, insofar as he has transgressed against the basic principles of the movement or has served its interests badly. Into his place then steps the better-able new man, however with equal authority and with equal responsibility.

[kap12:73]
It is one of the highest tasks of the movement to make this principle the determining one not only within its own rows, but also for the whole state.

[kap12:74]
Whoever wants to be leader bears, with highest unrestricted authority, also the last and heaviest responsibility.

[kap12:75]
Whoever is not able for that, or too cowardly for the bearing of the consequences of his doing, does not suit as leader. Only the hero is called to that.

[kap12:76]
The progress and the culture of mankind are not a product of the majority, but rest exclusively on the geniality and the deed-force of the personality.

[kap12:77]
To breed these up and to set them into their rights is one of the preconditions for the regaining of the greatness and power of our peoplehood.

[kap12:78]
Therewith the movement is, however, anti-parliamentary, and even its participation in a parliamentary institution can only possess the sense of an activity for its

[kap12:79]
smashing, for the removal of an institution in which we have to behold one of the heaviest decay-appearances of mankind.

[kap12:80]
Rejection of Religious Quarrels

[kap12:81]
10. The movement decidedly rejects every taking of position on questions which either lie outside the frame of its political work or are, as not of fundamental significance, irrelevant for it. Its task is not that of a religious reformation, but that of a political reorganization of our people. It sees in both religious confessions equally valuable supports for the continuance of our people and therefore fights those parties which want to degrade this foundation of a moral-religious and moral firming of our peoples-body into an instrument of their party-interests.

[kap12:82]
Neither Monarchic nor Republican

[kap12:83]
The movement finally sees its task not in the restoration of a determined state-form and in the struggle against another, but in the creation of those fundamental foundations without which on duration neither republic nor monarchy can exist. Its mission lies not in the founding of a monarchy or the firming of a republic, but in the creation of a Germanic state.

[kap12:84]
The question of the outer formation of this state, therefore its crowning, is not of fundamental significance, but is only conditioned through questions of practical expediency.

[kap12:85]
With a people which has first grasped the great problems and tasks of its existence, the questions of outer formalities will no longer lead to inner struggles.

[kap12:86]
The Necessary Evil of the Organization

[kap12:87]
11. The question of the inner organization of the movement is one of expediency and not of principle.

[kap12:88]
The best organization is not that which inserts the greatest mediator-apparatus between the leadership of a movement and the individual adherents, but that which inserts the smallest. For the task of the organization is the mediation of a determined idea — which first always springs from the head of an individual — to a plurality of men, as well as the supervision of its transformation into reality.

[kap12:89]
The organization is therewith in everything and each only a necessary evil. It is in the best case a means to the purpose, in the worst case purpose in itself.

[kap12:90]
Since the world brings forth more mechanical natures than ideal ones, the forms of the organization are wont mostly to form themselves more easily than ideas in themselves.

[kap12:91]
The course of every idea striving after realization, particularly with reformative character, is in great strokes the following:

[kap12:92]
Some genial thought arises in the brain of a man who feels himself called to mediate his knowledge to the rest of mankind. He preaches his view and gradually wins a determined circle of adherents. Intolerant Fanaticism

The First Great Mass-Assembly

[kap12:211]
At the beginning of the year 1920 I drove to the holding of the first quite great mass-assembly. Over that it came to differences of opinion. Some leading party-members held the thing for much too early and therewith in the effect for fateful. The red press had begun to busy itself with us, and we were happy enough gradually to earn its hatred. We had begun to step up as discussion-speakers in other assemblies. Naturally each of us was at once screamed down. Alone a success was yet present. One got to know us, and in just the measure in which this knowledge deepened, the aversion and rage against us rose. So we might well hope, at our first great mass-assembly, to receive the visit of our friends out of the red camp in the greatest extent.

[kap12:212]
Also I was clear to myself that the probability of a bursting was great. Alone the struggle just had to be fought out, if not now, then some months later. It lay wholly with us already on the first day to eternalize the movement through blind, ruthless standing-in for it. I knew above all the mentality of the adherents of the red sides only too well not to know that a resistance to the utmost most readily not only awakens impression, but also wins adherents. To this resistance one just had to be resolved.

[kap12:213]
The then first chairman of the party, Herr Harrer, believed he could not agree with my views in regard to the chosen point of time and in consequence stepped back, as an honest, upright man, from the leadership of the movement. [kap12:214]
So there was determined as term for the holding of this first great peoples-assembly of the still unknown movement the 24th of February 1920.

[kap12:215]

First Explanation of the Programme

[kap12:237]
At 7.30 o'clock the opening was to take place. At 7.15 o'clock I entered the festival-hall of the Hofbräuhaus at the Platzl in Munich, and my heart almost wanted to burst with joy. The mighty room — for mighty it appeared to me then still — was overfilled with men, head at head, a mass counting almost two thousand men. And above all — they had come to whom we wanted to turn. Far over the half of the hall seemed occupied by Communists and Independents. Our first great proclamation had been determined by them to a quick end.
