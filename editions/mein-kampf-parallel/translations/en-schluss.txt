On 9 November 1923, in the fourth year of its continuance, the National Socialist German Workers' Party was dissolved and forbidden for the whole Reich territory. Today, in November 1926, it stands free again in the whole Reich before us, stronger and inwardly firmer than ever before.

[schluss:1]
All persecutions of the movement and of its individual leaders, all slanderings and defamations were able to do nothing to it. The rightness of its ideas, the purity of its willing, the sacrifice-readiness of its adherents have hitherto let it come forth out of all oppressions stronger than ever.

[schluss:2]
If in the world of our today's parliamentary corruption it ever more recollects itself on the deepest being of its struggle and feels itself as pure embodiment of the value of race and person and orders itself accordingly, it will, on the ground of an almost mathematical lawfulness, one day carry off the victory in its struggle. Just as Germany must necessarily win the position due to it on this earth, if it is led and organized according to equal basic principles.

[schluss:3]
A state which in the age of race-poisoning devotes itself to the care of its best racial elements must one day become lord of the earth.

[schluss:4]
That may the adherents of our movement never forget, if ever the greatness of the sacrifices should tempt to the anxious comparison with the possible success.
