On 1 April 1924, by virtue of the verdict pronounced that day by the Munich People's Court, I had to begin my fortress confinement at Landsberg am Lech.

[vorwort:1]
Thereby, after years of uninterrupted work, the possibility offered itself to me for the first time of approaching a work that was demanded by many and felt by myself to be expedient for the movement. So I resolved, in two volumes, not only to set forth the aims of our movement but also to draw a picture of the development of the same.

[vorwort:2]
From it, more will be to be learned than from any purely doctrinal treatise.

[vorwort:3]
In doing so, I also had the opportunity to give an account of my own becoming, insofar as this is necessary for the understanding of both the first and the second volume, and can serve toward the destruction of the base manufacture of legends about my person carried on by the Jewish press.

[vorwort:4]
With this work I turn not to strangers, but to those followers of the movement who belong to it with their hearts and whose understanding now strives after more inward enlightenment.

[vorwort:5]
I know that one can win men less by the written word than, rather, by the spoken, that every great movement on this earth owes its growth to the great speakers and not to the great writers.

[vorwort:6]
Nevertheless, for the even and uniform representation of a doctrine, the fundamental of the same must be laid down for ever. To that end these two volumes shall count as building stones, which I add to the common work.
