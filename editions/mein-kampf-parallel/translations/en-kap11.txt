[kap11:0]
There are truths which lie so much in the street that precisely therefore they are not seen, or at least not recognized, by the ordinary world. It sometimes walks past such truisms as if blind, and is astonished in the highest degree when suddenly someone discovers what yet all would have to know. The eggs of Columbus lie around by the hundred-thousands, only the Columbuses are just rarer to find.

[kap11:1]
So men wander without exception about in the garden of nature, imagine that they know and understand almost everything, and yet walk, with few exceptions, as if blind past one of the most salient basic principles of its governance: the inner closedness of the species of all living beings of this earth.

[kap11:2]
Already the superficial contemplation shows, as an almost iron basic law of all the countless forms of expression of nature's will to live, its in-itself limited form of reproduction and multiplication. Every animal mates only with a companion of the same species. Titmouse goes to titmouse, finch to finch, the stork to the she-stork, field-mouse to field-mouse, house-mouse to house-mouse, the wolf to the she-wolf, etc.

[kap11:3]
Only extraordinary circumstances are able to change this, in the first line the compulsion of captivity as well as another impossibility of mating within the same species. But then nature begins also to brace herself against it with all means, and her most visible protest consists either in the refusal of further procreative capacity for the bastards, or she restricts the fertility of the later descendants; in most cases, however, she robs the resistance-capacity against sickness or hostile attacks.

[kap11:4]
That is only too natural.

[kap11:5]
The Race

[kap11:6]
Every crossing of two beings not wholly equally high gives as product a middle-thing between the height of the two parents. That means then: the young will stand higher than the racially lower half of the parental pair, alone not so high as the higher. Consequently it will later succumb in the struggle against this higher one. Such a mating, however, contradicts the will of nature to the higher-breeding of life in general. The precondition for this lies not in the joining of higher and more inferior, but in the unrestricted victory of the former. The stronger has to rule and not to merge with the weaker, so as to sacrifice his own greatness thereby. Only the born weakling can feel this as cruel, but for that he is also only a weak and limited man; for if this law did not rule, every conceivable higher development of all organic living beings would be unthinkable.

[kap11:7]
The consequence of this urge toward race-purity, generally valid in nature, is not only the sharp delimitation of the individual races toward the outside, but also their uniform nature-kind in themselves. The fox is always a fox, the goose a goose, the tiger a tiger, etc., and the difference can lie at most in the different measure of the strength, the power, the cleverness, adroitness, endurance, etc., of the individual specimens. But there will never be a fox to be found who according to his inner disposition could have, say, humane stirrings toward geese, as there is likewise no cat with friendly affection for mice.

[kap11:8]
Therefore the struggle among one another here also arises less in consequence of inner aversion, say, than rather from hunger and love. In both cases nature looks on calmly, indeed contentedly. The struggle for the daily bread lets everything weak and sickly, less resolute, succumb, while the struggle of the males for the female grants only to the healthiest the procreation-right or at least the possibility of it. Always, however, the struggle is a means for the promotion of the health and resistance-force of the species and hence a cause of the higher development of the same.

[kap11:9]
If the process were another, every further- and higher-formation would cease and rather the opposite would enter. For since the inferior in number always preponderates over against the best, the worse would, at equal preservation of life and reproduction-possibility, multiply itself so much more quickly

[kap11:10]
multiply, that finally the best would necessarily have to step into the background. A correction in favour of the better must therefore be undertaken. This, however, nature provides, in that she subjects the weaker part to such heavy life-conditions that already through them the number is limited, but the remainder finally she does not admit indiscriminately to multiplication, but here makes a new, ruthless selection according to strength and health.

[kap11:11]
As little, however, as she already wishes a mating of weaker individual beings with stronger, so much less still the fusion of a higher race with a lower, since otherwise her whole other, perhaps millennia-long work of higher-breeding would at one blow be void again. Historical experience offers countless proofs for this. It shows in frightening clearness that with every blood-mixing of the Aryan with lower peoples, as result the end of the culture-bearer came out. North America, whose population consists to by far the greatest part of Germanic elements, which mixed themselves only very little with lower coloured peoples, shows another mankind and culture than Central and South America, in which the mainly Romance immigrants had mingled themselves in sometimes large extent with the original inhabitants. Already at this one example one is able to recognize the effect of race-mixing clearly and distinctly. The racially purely and unmixedly remained Germane of the American continent has risen to be lord of the same; he will remain the lord so long as he does not himself fall victim to blood-shame.

[kap11:12]
The Result of Race-Crossing

[kap11:13]
The result of every race-crossing is therefore, quite briefly said, always the following:

[kap11:14]
a) Lowering-down of the level of the higher race,

[kap11:15]
b) bodily and spiritual decline and with it the beginning of a, if slow, yet surely progressing sickliness.

[kap11:16]
To bring about such a development means, however, indeed nothing other than to commit sin against the will of the eternal Creator.

[kap11:17]
As sin, however, this deed is also rewarded.

[kap11:18]
In that man attempts to rear himself up against the iron logic of nature, he gets into struggle with the basic principles to which also he himself alone owes his existence as man. So his action against nature must lead to his own downfall.

[kap11:19]
Here, to be sure, comes the truly Jewishly impudent, but just as stupid, objection of the modern pacifist: "Man just overcomes nature!"

[kap11:20]
Millions prattle this Jewish nonsense thoughtlessly after, and in the end really imagine that they themselves represent a kind of nature-overcomers; whereby, however, there stands at their disposal as weapon nothing further than an idea, and moreover one so miserable that after it really no world could be imagined.

[kap11:21]
Alone quite apart from the fact that man has not yet overcome nature in a single thing, but at most snatched and tried to lift up the one or the other little tip of her enormous, giant-like veil of eternal riddles and secrets, that he in truth invents nothing but discovers everything only, that he does not rule nature but has only, on the ground of the knowledge of individual natural laws and secrets, risen to be lord of those other living beings to whom this knowledge is just lacking — quite apart from that, then, an idea cannot overcome the preconditions for the becoming and being of mankind, since the idea itself depends only on man. Without men there is no human idea in this world; hence the idea as such is indeed always conditioned by the presence of men and with it of all the laws which created the precondition for this existence.

[kap11:22]
Man and Idea

[kap11:23]
And not only that! Determined ideas are even bound to determined men. This holds most of all precisely for such thoughts whose content has its origin not in an exact scientific truth, but in the world of feeling or, as one today is pleased to express oneself so beautifully and clearly, reproduces an "inner experience". All these ideas, which have nothing to do with cold logic in itself but represent pure expressions of feeling, ethical conceptions, etc., are fettered to the existence of the men whose spiritual imagination- and creator-force they owe their own existence. Precisely then, however, the preservation of these determined races and men is the precondition for the continuance of these ideas. Whoever, e.g., wanted really from the heart to wish the victory of the pacifist thought in this world would have to set himself with all means for the conquest of the world by the Germans; for if it should come the other way round, with the last German very easily also the last pacifist would die out, since the other world has hardly ever fallen so deeply for this nature- and reason-contrary nonsense as unfortunately our own people. One would thus willy-nilly, with earnest will, have to decide to lead wars, in order to come to pacifism. This and nothing else had the American world-redeemer Wilson also intended — so at least believed our German fantastics — whereby then the purpose was reached.

[kap11:24]
In fact the pacifist-humane idea is perhaps quite good then, when the highest-standing man has beforehand conquered and subjected the world in an extent which makes him the sole lord of this earth. This idea then lacks the possibility of a harmful effect in just the measure in which its practical application becomes rare and finally impossible. So first struggle and then perhaps pacifism. In the other case mankind has overshot the summit of its development, and the end is not the rule of some ethical idea, but barbarism and in consequence chaos. Here, of course, the one or the other may laugh; alone this planet already drew for millions of years through the ether without men, and it can one day draw so again, if men forget that they owe their higher existence not to the ideas of some mad ideologues but to the knowledge and ruthless application of iron laws of nature.

[kap11:25]
Race and Culture

[kap11:26]
Everything that we today admire on this earth — science and art, technology and inventions — is only the creative product of few peoples and perhaps originally of one race. On them also depends the continuance of this whole culture. If they perish, the beauty of this earth sinks with them into the grave.

[kap11:27]
However much the soil, for example, may be able to influence men, yet the result of the influence will always be different, according to the races coming into consideration. The low fertility of a living-space may spur the one race to highest achievements; with another it will only be the cause of bitterest poverty and final undernourishment with all its consequences. Always the inner disposition of the peoples is determining for the kind of effect of outer influences. What with the ones leads to starving educates the others to hard work.

[kap11:28]
All great cultures of the past perished only because the originally creative race died away from blood-poisoning.

[kap11:29]
Always the last cause of such a downfall was the forgetting that all culture depends on men and not the reverse, that therefore, in order to preserve a determined culture, the man who created it must be preserved.

[kap11:30]
This preservation, however, is bound to the iron law of necessity and of the right of the victory of the best and strongest.

[kap11:31]
Whoever wants to live, then, let him struggle, and whoever does not want to contend in this world of eternal wrestling does not deserve life.

[kap11:32]
Even if this were hard — it is just so! Surely, however, by far the hardest fate is that which meets the man who believes he can overcome nature and in the ground taken yet only mocks her. Need, misfortune and sicknesses are then her answer!

[kap11:33]
The man who misjudges and disregards the race-laws really robs himself of the happiness which appears destined for him. He hinders the victory-procession of the best race and with it, however, also the precondition of all human progress. He betakes himself in consequence, burdened with the sensitivity of man, into the realm of the helpless animal.

[kap11:34]
The Aryan as Culture-Founder

[kap11:35]
It is an idle beginning to quarrel over which race or races were the original bearers of human culture and therewith the real founders of what we comprehend altogether with the word mankind. It is simpler to pose this question for the present, and here the answer also results easily and clearly. What we today see before us of human culture, of results of art, science and technology, is almost exclusively creative product of the Aryan. Precisely this fact, however, lets the not unfounded inference be drawn that he alone was the founder of higher mankind in general, hence represents the prime-type of what we understand under the word "man". He is the Prometheus of mankind, from whose light forehead the divine spark of genius leapt forth at all times, always anew kindling that fire which, as knowledge, brightened the night of silent secrets and so let man ascend the way to the ruler of the other beings of this earth. Switch him off — and deep darkness will perhaps already after few millennia sink down again upon the earth, human culture would pass away and the world become desolate.

[kap11:36]
If one would divide mankind into three kinds: into culture-founders, culture-bearers and culture-destroyers, then as representative of the first presumably only the Aryan would come into question. From him stem the foundations and walls of all human creations, and only the outer form and colour are conditioned by the respective character-traits of the individual peoples. He delivers the mighty building-stones and plans for all human progress, and only the execution corresponds to the nature-kind of the respective races. In few decades, for example, the whole East of Asia will call a culture its own whose last foundation will be just as Hellenic spirit and Germanic technology as is the case with us. Only the outer form will — in part at least — bear the traits of Asiatic nature-kind. It is not so, as some think, that Japan takes European technology to its culture, but the European science and technology is trimmed with Japanese peculiarities. The foundation of the actual life is no longer the particular Japanese culture, although it — because outwardly, in consequence of the inner difference, springing more into the eyes for the European — determines the colour of life, but the mighty scientific-technical work

[kap11:37]
of Europe and America, therefore of Aryan peoples. On these achievements alone can also the East follow the general human progress. This yields the foundation of the struggle for the daily bread, creates weapons and tools for it, and only the outer arrangement will gradually be adapted to the Japanese being.

[kap11:38]
If from today every further Aryan influence on Japan were to fail to appear — assuming Europe and America perish — then for a short time still the today's ascent of Japan in science and technology could hold on; alone already in few years the well would run dry, the Japanese peculiarity gain, but the today's culture stiffen and sink back again into the sleep from which it was startled up seventy years ago by the Aryan culture-wave. Therefore, just as the today's Japanese development owes its life to Aryan origin, also once in grey past foreign influence and foreign spirit was the awakener of the then Japanese culture. The best proof for this is delivered by the fact of the later ossification and complete stiffening of the same. It can with a people only enter when the originally creative race-core was lost, or the outer influence later was lacking, which gave the impulse and the material for the first development in the cultural field. If, however, it stands firm that a people receives, takes up and works up its culture in the most essential basic materials from foreign races, in order then, after the failing of further outer influence, always again to stiffen, one can call such a race well a "culture-bearing" one, but never a "culture-creative" one.

[kap11:39]
An examination of the individual peoples from this viewpoint yields the fact that it is almost throughout not a matter of originally culture-founding ones, but almost always of culture-bearing ones.

[kap11:40]
Always there results about the following picture of their development:

[kap11:41]
Aryan tribes subject — often in truly ridiculously small people-number — foreign peoples and now develop, stimulated by the particular life-relations of the new territory (fertility, climatic conditions, etc.) and favoured by the crowd of help-forces of men of lower kind standing at disposal, their spiritual and organizational abilities slumbering in them. They create in often few millennia, indeed centuries, cultures which originally bear completely the inner traits of their being, adapted to the above already indicated particular

[kap11:42]
properties of the soil as well as of the subjected men. Finally, however, the conquerors transgress against the principle, observed in the beginning, of the keeping-pure of their blood, begin to mix themselves with the subjugated inhabitants and therewith end their own existence; for the Fall in Paradise was still always followed by the expulsion from the same.

[kap11:43]
After a thousand years and more there then often shows itself the last visible trace of the once master-people in the lighter skin-tone which its blood left behind to the subjected race, and in a stiffened culture which it as original creatoress once had founded. For just as the actual and spiritual conqueror was lost in the blood of the subjected, so also the fuel for the torch of human culture-progress was lost! As the colour kept, through the blood of the former lords, a soft shimmer as remembrance of these, so also the night of cultural life is mildly brightened through the remained creations of the once light-bringers. They shine through all the barbarism that has come again, and awake in the thoughtless beholder of the moment only too often the opinion that he sees the picture of the present people before him, while it is only the mirror of the past into which he looks.

[kap11:44]
It can then occur that such a people comes a second time, indeed even still more often, during its history into contact with the race of its once culture-bringers, without a remembrance of earlier encounters needing still to be present. Unconsciously the rest of the once master-blood will turn toward the new appearance, and what first was possible only to compulsion can now succeed to its own will. A new culture-wave holds its entry and lasts so long until its bearers perish again in the blood of foreign peoples.

[kap11:45]
It will be the task of a future culture- and world-history to research in this sense and not to suffocate in the reproduction of outer facts, as unfortunately only too often is the case with our today's science of history.

[kap11:46]
Already from this sketch of the development of "culture-bearing" nations there results, however, also the picture of the becoming, working and — passing-away of the true culture-founders of this earth, the Aryans themselves.

[kap11:47]
Just as in daily life the so-called genius needs a particular occasion, indeed often a formal impulse, in order to be brought to shining, so in the life of peoples also the genial race. In the monotony of the everyday, often also

[kap11:48]
significant men are wont to appear insignificant and hardly to tower above the average of their surroundings; as soon, however, as a situation approaches them in which others would despair or go astray, there grows visibly forth from the inconspicuous average-child the genial nature, not seldom to the astonishment of all those who saw it hitherto in the smallness of bourgeois life — therefore, then, the prophet in his own land is seldom wont to count for something. To observe this, one has nowhere more opportunity than in war. Out of seemingly harmless children there shoot suddenly, in hours of need, when others despair, heroes forth, of death-defying resolution and icy coldness of deliberation. If this hour of trial had not come, hardly anyone would have suspected that in the beardless boy a young hero is hidden. Almost always it needs some impulse to call the genius onto the plan. The hammer-stroke of fate which strikes the one to the ground strikes with the other suddenly upon steel, and in that the shell of the everyday breaks, there lies before the eyes of the astonished world the hitherto hidden core openly exposed. This world then braces itself and will not believe it that the kind seemingly equal to it should suddenly be another being; a process which indeed repeats itself with every significant human child.

[kap11:49]
Although an inventor, for example, founds his fame only on the day of his invention, yet it is erroneous to think that also geniality in itself would only at this hour have driven into the man — the spark of genius is present since the hour of birth in the forehead of the truly creatively disposed man. True geniality is always inborn and never educated-to or even learned-on.

[kap11:50]
This holds, however, as already stressed, not only for the individual man, but also for the race. Creatively active peoples are from ever and from the ground out creatively disposed, even if this should not be recognizable to the eyes of superficial beholders. Also here the outer recognition is always only possible in the following of accomplished deeds, since the other world is indeed not able to recognize geniality in itself, but only sees its visible expressions in the form of inventions, discoveries, buildings, pictures, etc.; but also here it often still takes a long time until it is able to wrestle itself through to this knowledge. Just as in the life of the individual significant man the genial or yet extraordinary disposition, first driven on by particular occasions, strives after its practical

[kap11:51]
realization, so also in the life of peoples the real utilization of present creative forces and abilities can often only take place when determined preconditions invite to it.

[kap11:52]
Most clearly we see this at the race which was and is the bearer of human culture-development — at the Aryans. As soon as fate leads them toward particular relations, their present abilities begin to develop in ever quicker succession and to pour themselves into tangible forms. The cultures which they found in such cases are almost always decisively determined by the present soil, the given climate and — the subjected men. This last, however, is almost the most decisive. The more primitive the technical preconditions for a culture-activity are, the more necessary is the presence of human help-forces, which then, organizationally gathered together and applied, have to replace the force of the machine. Without this possibility of the use of lower men, the Aryan would never have been able to take the first steps to his later culture; just as, without the help of individual suitable animals which he understood to tame for himself, he would not have come to a technology which now just lets him slowly dispense with these animals. The word: "The Moor has done his duty, the Moor can go" has unfortunately its only too deep meaning. For millennia the horse had to serve man and help along to lay the foundations of a development which now, in consequence of the motor-car, makes the horse itself superfluous. In few years it will have ceased its activity; alone without its earlier cooperation man would perhaps only with difficulty have come to where he is today.

[kap11:53]
So for the formation of higher cultures the presence of lower men was one of the most essential preconditions, in that only they were able to replace the lack of technical auxiliary means, without which, however, a higher development is not at all thinkable. Surely the first culture of mankind rested less on the tamed animal than rather on the use of lower men.

[kap11:54]
Only after the enslavement of subjected races did the same fate begin also to meet animals, and not the reverse, as some may well believe. For first the defeated went before the plough — and only after him the horse. Only pacifist fools, however, are able to see this again as a sign of human depravity, without becoming clear to themselves that

[kap11:55]
this development just had to take place, in order finally to arrive at the place from where today these apostles can set their blather into the world.

[kap11:56]
The progress of mankind resembles the ascent on an endless ladder; one just does not come higher without first having taken the lower steps. So the Aryan had to stride the way which reality pointed out to him, and not the one of which the fantasy of a modern pacifist dreams. The way of reality, however, is hard and heavy; alone it leads finally to where the other would gladly dream men to, from where, however, he in truth rather removes them still more than that he brings them nearer.

[kap11:57]
It is therefore no accident that the first cultures arose there where the Aryan, in the meeting with lower peoples, subjected these and made them subject to his will. They were then the first technical instrument in the service of a becoming culture.

[kap11:58]
Consequences of the Blood-Mixing

[kap11:59]
Therewith, however, the way which the Aryan had to go was clearly pre-drawn. As conqueror he subjected the lower men and then regulated their practical activity under his command, according to his willing and for his goals. Alone in that he so led them to a useful, if hard, activity, he not only spared the life of the subjected, but gave them perhaps even a lot which was better than that of their former so-called "freedom". So long as he ruthlessly upheld the master-standpoint, he remained not only really the master, but also the preserver and increaser of culture. For this rested exclusively on his abilities and therewith on his preservation in itself. As soon as the subjected began to lift themselves and probably also linguistically approached the conqueror, the sharp dividing wall between master and servant fell. The Aryan gave up the purity of his blood and lost for it the stay in the Paradise which he had created for himself. He sank down in the race-mixing, gradually lost ever more his cultural ability, until he finally began to resemble, not only spiritually but also bodily, the subjected and the original inhabitants more than his forefathers. For a time he could still live on the present culture-goods; then, however, stiffening entered, and he finally fell to forgetfulness.

[kap11:60]
So cultures and empires break together, in order to make the place free for new formations.

[kap11:61]
The blood-mixing and the thereby conditioned sinking of the race-level is the sole cause of the dying-away of all cultures; for men do not perish from lost wars, but from the loss of that resistance-force which is proper to pure blood alone.

[kap11:62]
What is not good race on this world is chaff.

[kap11:63]
All world-historical happening, however, is only the expression of the self-preservation-urge of the races in the good or bad sense.

[kap11:64]
Causes of the Significance of the Aryan

[kap11:65]
The question after the inner causes of the towering significance of Aryandom can be answered to the effect that these are to be sought less in a stronger disposition of the self-preservation-urge in itself than rather in the particular kind of the expression of the same. The will to life is, subjectively considered, everywhere equally great and only different in the form of the actual effect.

[kap11:66]
With the most original living beings the self-preservation-urge does not go out over the care for one's own ego. The egoism, as we designate this addiction, goes here so far that it comprises even time itself, so that the moment itself again claims everything and wants to grant nothing to the coming hours. The animal lives in this state only for itself, seeks fodder only for the respective hunger and struggles only for its own life. So long, however, as the self-preservation-urge expresses itself in this manner, every foundation for the formation of a commonwealth is lacking, and were it even the most primitive form of the family. Already the community between male and female beyond the pure mating demands an extension of the self-preservation-urge, in that the care and the struggle for one's own ego turns also to the second part; the male sometimes seeks fodder also for the female, but mostly both seek nourishment for the young. For the protection of the one, almost always the other steps in, so that here the first, if infinitely simple, forms of a sacrifice-sense result. As soon as this sense extends itself over the limits of the narrow frame of the family, there results the precondition for the formation of larger associations and finally formal states.

[kap11:67]
With the lowest men of the earth this property is present only in very small extent, so that it does not come out over the formation of the family. The greater then the readiness of the putting-back of purely personal interests becomes, the more also rises the ability for the erection of comprehensive commonwealths.

[kap11:68]
Service to the Community

[kap11:69]
This sacrifice-will, to the employment of personal work and, if need be, of one's own life for others, is most strongly developed with the Aryan. The Aryan is not greatest in his spiritual properties in themselves, but in the measure of the readiness to put all abilities into the service of the community. The self-preservation-urge has with him reached the noblest form, in that he willingly subordinates his own ego to the life of the totality and, if the hour demands it, also brings it to sacrifice.

[kap11:70]
Not in the intellectual gifts lies the cause of the culture-forming and -building ability of the Aryan. If he had only these alone, he could with them always only work destructively, on no account, however, organizingly; for the innermost being of every organization rests on this, that the individual renounces the representation of his personal opinion as well as of his interests and sacrifices both in favour of a plurality of men. Only over the detour of this generality does he then receive his part back again. He works now, e.g., no longer immediately for himself, but incorporates himself with his activity into the frame of the totality, not only to his own use, but to the use of all. The most wonderful elucidation of this disposition offers his word "work", under which he understands by no means an activity for the preservation of life in itself, but only a creating which does not contradict the interests of the generality. In the other case he designates human working, so far as it serves the self-preservation-urge without regard to the welfare of the with-world, as theft, usury, robbery, burglary, etc.

[kap11:71]
This disposition, which lets the interest of one's own ego step back in favour of the preservation of the community, is really the first precondition for every truly human culture. Only out of it can all the great works of mankind arise, which bring the founder little wage, but posterity the richest blessing. Yes, out of it alone one can understand how so many are able to bear a meagre life in honesty, which imposes on themselves only poverty and modesty, but secures to the totality the foundations of existence. Every worker, every peasant, every inventor, official, etc., who creates without ever being able to come himself to happiness and prosperity, is a bearer of this high idea, even if the deeper sense of his action remained forever hidden to him.

[kap11:72]
Sacrifice-Readiness for the Totality

[kap11:73]
What, however, holds for work as foundation of human nourishment and of all human progress meets in still higher measure for the protection of man and his culture. In the devotion of one's own life for the existence of the community lies the crowning of all sacrifice-sense. Only thereby is it hindered that what men's hands built, men's hands again overthrow or nature destroys.

[kap11:74]
Precisely our German language, however, possesses a word which in glorious manner designates the action according to this sense: fulfilment of duty — that is, not to suffice oneself, but to serve the generality.

[kap11:75]
The fundamental disposition out of which such an action grows we call, in distinction from egoism, from self-interest — idealism. We understand under it only the sacrifice-readiness of the individual for the totality, for his fellow-men.

[kap11:76]
Purest Idealism, Deepest Knowledge

[kap11:77]
How necessary it is, however, ever again to recognize that idealism does not represent, say, a superfluous expression of feeling, but that it in truth was, is and will be the precondition for what we designate with human culture, indeed that it alone first created the concept "man". To this inner disposition the Aryan owes his position on this world, and to it the world owes man; for it alone has, out of pure spirit, formed the creative force which in unique wedlock of raw fist and genial intellect created the monuments of human culture.

[kap11:78]
Without his ideal disposition all, even the most dazzling, abilities of the spirit would be only spirit in itself, outer seeming without inner value, but never creative force.

[kap11:79]
Since, however, true idealism is nothing further than the subordination of the interests and of the life of the individual under the totality, this, however, again represents the precondition for the formation of organizational forms of every kind, it corresponds in the innermost ground to the last willing of nature. It alone leads men to the voluntary recognition of the prerogative of force and strength and lets them so become a mote of that order which forms and builds the whole universe.

[kap11:80]
Purest idealism covers itself unconsciously with deepest knowledge.

[kap11:81]
How much this is true, and how little true idealism has to do with playful fantasticality, one can recognize at once if one lets the unspoiled child, the healthy boy, e.g., judge. The same boy who stands understandinglessly and rejecting toward the tirades of an "ideal" pacifist is ready to throw away his young life for the ideal of his peoplehood.

[kap11:82]
Here the instinct of the knowledge of the deeper necessity of the preservation of the species obeys unconsciously, if need be at the cost of the individual, and protests against the fantasticality of the pacifist babbler, who in truth, as a, if rouged, yet cowardly egoist transgresses against the laws of development; for this is conditioned by the sacrifice-readiness of the individual in favour of the generality and not by sickly conceptions of cowardly know-betters and critics of nature.

[kap11:83]
